= The Automation Landscape — a Survey Grounding the Meta-Model
:toc:
:toclevels: 3

== Purpose and method

link:../../decisions/0002-automation-interlingua-declarative-imperative.adoc[ADR-0002]
commits HAR to an interlingua whose schema is a *tool-neutral meta-model of
automation* (`Resource`, `Effect`, `Dependency`, `State`, `Owner`, `Provider`,
`HandoffBoundary`, `Provenance`). A meta-model is only as good as the survey
of what it must be neutral *over*. This document is that survey.

It is deliberately ordered from broad to specific:

. *Part I — Foundations*: the shape of the whole space, with particular
  attention to the three cross-cutting engines that recur everywhere —
  *solvers* (finding a plan), *provers* (certifying a plan), and the
  *centralisation of language toolchains* (a live, already-successful
  miniature of the N-front-ends + M-back-ends argument).
. *Part II — The package-management surface*: the concrete operational
  functionality of mature single-host tools — `apt`, `aptitude`, `nala`,
  `dnf`, `zypper` — because they are the oldest, most battle-tested
  declarative/imperative hybrids in existence and their feature set is a
  checklist the meta-model must be able to express.
. *Part III — Enterprise automation*: provisioning, configuration
  management, orchestration, and harnesses — the fleet-scale tools whose
  fragmentation is the problem ADR-0002 exists to address, and the source of
  the meta-model's ownership/handoff/provenance concepts.
. *Part IV — Consequences*: what the survey forces the meta-model to
  contain, expressed as mappings and design seams.

Scope note: per ADR-0002, this survey informs translation and handoff
(Phases 2A/2B). It is *not* a competitive analysis for replacing any tool
listed; replacement is out of scope and sponsor-gated.

Companion:
link:../../decisions/0003-install-domain-staged-subsumption-ladder.adoc[ADR-0003]
selects software installation & package management as the *first concrete
resource domain* (Phase 2C) and fixes a five-tier subsumption ladder for
absorbing it (manager-less installs first, incumbent managers last). This
survey is the *landscape grounding* that sits underneath that ladder: Part II
describes the functionality the ladder's tiers 3–5 must eventually subsume,
and Part I's foundations explain the machinery (solvers, provers, scoped
state) the meta-model needs before any tier is attempted.

== Part I — Foundations

=== 1.1 The shape of the space

Every automation tool, from `apt install` to a Kubernetes operator, performs
the same abstract cycle: *express intent → compare against observed reality →
compute a change set → effect it → record what happened*. The families differ
along a small number of axes:

[%autowidth]
|===
|Axis |Poles |Examples

|Control paradigm
|declarative ⇄ imperative
|Terraform HCL, Puppet catalogs ⇄ shell, Ansible task lists, HAR dispatch

|Scope of a "resource universe"
|single host ⇄ fleet ⇄ cloud estate
|apt ⇄ Puppet/Salt ⇄ Terraform

|Convergence style
|one-shot transaction ⇄ continuous reconciliation
|package transaction ⇄ Kubernetes controller loop

|Consistency discipline
|globally-consistent solve ⇄ pinned/content-addressed
|apt/dnf/zypper ⇄ Nix, Guix, lockfiles

|Who runs it
|human at a prompt ⇄ agent/daemon ⇄ CI harness
|nala ⇄ puppet agent ⇄ GitHub Actions
|===

The meta-model must place any tool on all five axes without distortion. The
rest of Part I examines the three machinery layers that cut *across* the
families.

=== 1.2 Solvers — lowering is constraint solving

ADR-0002 names *lowering* (declarative graph → imperative plan) as the
`terraform plan` move. The survey's first structural finding is that lowering
is, in the general case, *constraint solving*, and the difficulty is set by
the constraint language:

* *Package dependency resolution is NP-complete.* Dependencies plus conflicts
  plus version disjunctions encode Boolean satisfiability (established in the
  EDOS/Mancoosi research line on Debian-class distributions). Every serious
  package manager therefore ships or embeds a real solver:
** `apt` — a heuristic internal resolver; notably, apt also defines *EDSP*,
   an external-solver protocol through which the whole resolution step can be
   delegated to a genuine constraint solver (e.g. `aspcud`, Answer Set
   Programming, via `apt-cudf`). apt thus already treats "the solve" as a
   pluggable seam — precedent for the meta-model doing the same.
** `aptitude` — its own resolver that produces *multiple candidate
   solutions* and lets the user interactively accept, reject, and re-score
   them: human-in-the-loop solving, discussed further in Part II.
** `libsolv` (used by `dnf` and `zypper`) — a genuine SAT solver with
   CDCL-style learning under the hood.
** *PubGrub* (Dart's `pub`, since adopted by other resolvers such as `uv`) —
   a CDCL-family algorithm designed so that *unsatisfiability is explained in
   human-readable terms*. This matters: a solver embedded in an interlingua
   must be able to justify the plan it emits, or refusal to emit one.
** Cargo/npm-style resolvers — soften the problem by *allowing duplicate
   versions* in one graph, trading global consistency for tractability and
   artefact size.
* *Nix and Guix dissolve the solve* rather than winning it: content-addressed
  store paths mean two versions never conflict, so there is no global
  consistency constraint left to satisfy. Pinning replaces solving.
* *Terraform's DAG walk is the degenerate easy case*: no disjunction, no
  conflict — just topological sort with parallelism. Polynomial, which is why
  provisioning tools rarely talk about "solvers" at all.

*Consequences for the meta-model* — lowering must be a *pluggable solve step*
whose required power depends on the constraint vocabulary the graph uses
(mere `before`/`require` edges → toposort; version disjunction + conflict →
SAT-class); and emitted plans should carry the solver's *explanation* in
`Provenance`, because every mature solver ecosystem converged on
explainability (aptitude's interactive solutions, PubGrub's derivations,
apt's EDSP answers).

=== 1.3 Provers — from finding a plan to certifying it

A solver finds *a* plan; a prover certifies *properties* of plans and of the
operators they are built from. The properties that recur across the landscape
are exactly the ones the incumbent tools assert informally and almost never
check:

* *Idempotence* — running the plan twice equals running it once. Ansible
  modules, Chef resources, and Puppet types all *promise* this per-operator;
  none verify it mechanically. Research systems have shown it is checkable
  (e.g. the PLDI-line work on verifying determinism and idempotence of Puppet
  configurations), but nothing shipped in the mainstream tools.
* *Convergence* — repeated application reaches, and then remains at, the
  desired state. CFEngine's promise theory made this the organising idea of
  configuration management, but as an operational doctrine, not a checked
  theorem. Kubernetes reconcilers assume it by construction.
* *Exactly-once / non-duplication* — an effect is applied by precisely one
  owner. No mainstream tool states this across a *tool boundary*, because no
  mainstream tool has tool boundaries — which is precisely the handoff gap.
* *Non-interference* — two plans touching disjoint resources commute. Assumed
  silently by every parallel executor.

HAR's position, per ADR-0002, is that these obligations become
machine-checked at the meta-model layer: the *Idris2 normative spec* carries
idempotence/convergence invariants, *Ephapax linear types*
(`src/abi/LinearRouting.eph`) make exactly-once ownership across a
`HandoffBoundary` a type-level fact, and *proven-FSM* governs the per-resource
lifecycle (`declared → planned → realised → handed-off`). The survey's point
is that this is not gold-plating: every family below has a folk-theorem
version of these properties, and the interlingua is the first artefact
positioned where proving them pays — at the seam *between* tools, where no
single tool can.

=== 1.4 Language toolchain centralisation — a live miniature of the thesis

The most instructive recent development in the broad landscape is what
happened to *language toolchain management*, because it recapitulates
ADR-0002's N×M argument at small scale and has already played out:

. *Fragmentation phase* — every language grew its own version manager:
  `rustup`, `ghcup`, `nvm`, `pyenv`, `rbenv`, `sdkman`, `opam`, `juliaup`,
  `coursier`. N ecosystems × M shell-integration conventions, each with its
  own shims, env vars, and config file.
. *Centralisation phase* — `asdf` introduced a single manager with a *plugin
  interface*: any tool that can answer "list versions / install version V /
  set active version for scope S" becomes a plugin. Successors (`mise`,
  `proto`, `aqua`) kept the plugin ontology and improved the engine. This is
  an interlingua in miniature: a tiny tool-neutral meta-model (tool, version,
  scope, activation) onto which N ecosystems were mapped, collapsing N×M
  integrations to N+M. It worked, and it never needed to *replace* `rustup`
  or `nvm` — plugins often shell out to them.
. *Unification phase* — Nix/Guix dev environments, devcontainers, and Bazel
  toolchains go further: the toolchain becomes an ordinary declarative
  resource inside a general environment description, lockfile-pinned and
  content-addressed (`flake.lock`, Guix manifests + channels, `.tool-versions`
  / `mise.toml` at the modest end).

Three lessons transfer directly:

* A *small* meta-model suffices for a whole family if the verbs are chosen
  well — asdf's plugin ontology is four verbs.
* *Scope* is a first-class attribute of state (global vs project vs shell
  session); the meta-model's `State` must be scopable or the toolchain family
  cannot be expressed.
* Centralisers won by *wrapping*, not replacing, incumbents — the same
  "join up the fragments" posture ADR-0002 commits to.

== Part II — The package-management surface

Single-host package managers are the oldest declarative/imperative hybrids:
the *request* ("have `nginx` installed") is declarative; the *transaction*
that realises it is an ordered imperative script (unpack → configure in
dependency order). Their accumulated functionality is a checklist for the
meta-model. `nala` and `aptitude` get individual treatment because each
contributes a distinct idea. (In ADR-0003's ladder these managers sit at
tiers 3–5 — subsumed *last*, precisely so the meta-model is not built in
their image; this part records what they do, not a commitment to wrap them
early.)

=== 2.1 The common verb set

Across `apt`, `dnf`, `zypper` (and their kin) the stable operational surface
is:

[%autowidth]
|===
|Verb family |Examples |Meta-model reading

|State change
|`install`, `remove`, `purge`, `upgrade`, `full-upgrade`, `autoremove`
|declare desired `State` deltas on `Resource`s

|Query
|`search`, `show`, `list --installed`, `depends`, `rdepends`
|read the resource graph and observed `State`

|Plan preview
|`--dry-run` / `--simulate`, apt's "The following packages will be…" summary
|lowering exposed to the user *before* execution — every mature tool grew one

|Policy constraint
|`hold`, pinning/priorities, version locks, `forbid-version`
|constraints injected into the solve, distinct from desired state

|Acquisition
|`download`, mirror selection, parallel fetch
|`Provider` mechanics, separable from state change

|History
|`dnf history`, `zypper` + snapshots, `nala history`
|the plan journal — see 2.3/2.4
|===

The plan-preview row deserves emphasis: package managers independently
evolved the same "show the plan, then apply it" interaction that Terraform
made famous. The lowering artefact is *user-facing* in every mature tool.

=== 2.2 aptitude — the solver made conversational, and intent made explicit

`aptitude` contributes two ideas the meta-model should keep:

* *Interactive, steerable resolution.* When the dependency solve has no
  clean answer, aptitude enumerates candidate solutions ("remove X / hold Y /
  install Z from…") and lets the user accept, reject, or re-weight them,
  iterating until a solution is approved. That is human-in-the-loop
  constraint solving with the solver's alternatives as first-class,
  presentable objects — precisely the shape a HAR plan review/approval step
  should take, and a concrete argument for plans (and *rejected candidate
  plans*) being representable in the interchange dialect.
* *The auto/manual distinction.* Every installed package is marked either
  *manually requested* or *automatically installed to satisfy dependencies*;
  `autoremove` is then a mark-and-sweep garbage collection rooted at the
  manual marks. This is the cleanest existing implementation of a distinction
  the meta-model must carry: *directly-intended state* versus *derived state*
  that exists only in service of intent. In meta-model terms it is a
  `Provenance` fact ("why does this resource exist?") with operational
  consequences (derived resources are reclaimable when unreferenced).

Secondary but notable: aptitude's search-pattern language (`?depends(…)`,
`?broken`, `~n…`) is a query DSL over the resource graph — evidence that once
a resource graph exists, users demand a way to *query* it, which the
interlingua should anticipate rather than improvise later.

=== 2.3 nala — UX, history, and undo as product features

`nala` is a front-end over the same libapt machinery apt uses — same solver,
same archives — and is nonetheless widely adopted. What it adds is entirely
in presentation and record-keeping:

* *Readable transactions* — side-by-side, colour-coded summaries of what a
  transaction will do; the lowering artefact rendered for humans.
* *Parallel downloads and mirror ranking* (`nala fetch`) — `Provider`
  optimisation without touching resolution semantics.
* *`nala history`, with `undo`/`redo`* — every transaction is journalled with
  an ID and can be replayed or inverted. Undo of a package transaction is
  *inverse-plan synthesis*: compute the plan that returns to the prior state.
  That is a restricted, concrete instance of ADR-0002's *lifting* problem
  (action sequence → state delta → inverse actions), working in production on
  the easiest domain.

The lesson is sharp because nala adds *zero* solving power: history,
provenance, and plan presentation are not garnish — they are sufficient
reason for a tool to exist. The interchange dialect should treat the journal
and the human-renderable plan as first-class outputs, not debug artefacts.

=== 2.4 Transactionality and rollback

The family also maps the spectrum of *recovery* semantics the meta-model must
express:

* `apt` — no native transaction history; failed mid-transaction states are
  repaired forward (`dpkg --configure -a`), not rolled back.
* `dnf history rollback` / `undo` — journal-based inverse plans (nala-style,
  older pedigree).
* `zypper` with snapper/btrfs — filesystem snapshots bracket transactions:
  checkpoint-based rollback rather than inverse plans.
* `transactional-update`, `rpm-ostree` — the transaction builds a new
  immutable image/snapshot and switches atomically; rollback is a pointer
  flip. Convergence by replacement rather than mutation — the single-host
  analogue of immutable infrastructure.

These are four distinct answers to "what if the plan fails halfway?" —
repair-forward, inverse plan, checkpoint restore, atomic switch. Handoff
(2B) needs checkpoints anyway (`HandoffBoundary` is defined as *linear
ownership transfer with a checkpoint*), so the meta-model gets rollback
vocabulary almost for free — but it must not hard-code *one* of the four
semantics, because the surveyed tools legitimately differ.

== Part III — Enterprise automation

=== 3.1 Provisioning

*Terraform / OpenTofu* is the reference point, and its anatomy maps onto the
meta-model with almost embarrassing directness:

* the *state file* is a persistent record of *realised* `State` (distinct
  from both desired-in-code and observed-in-cloud — three states, exactly the
  desired/observed/realised triple in the meta-model);
* `plan` is lowering, `apply` is execution, drift detection is the
  observed-vs-recorded diff;
* *providers* are back-end adapters — the M side of N×M;
* `import` is *lifting* — adopting an existing unmanaged resource into
  desired state — and its notorious awkwardness (one resource at a time,
  attributes reconstructed imperfectly) is field evidence for ADR-0002's
  warning that lifting is lossy and hard;
* the state file is also Terraform's biggest operational liability (locking,
  secrets, team contention) — a warning about what a naïvely centralised
  realised-state record becomes at fleet scale.

Around it: *Pulumi* demonstrates the duality inside one tool — imperative
general-purpose languages *construct* a declarative resource graph, which is
then planned and applied like Terraform's; the paradigm boundary is not a
tool boundary. *CloudFormation/Bicep* show hosted, service-side lowering.
*Packer* and *cloud-init* cover the imperative edges of provisioning (image
bake, first boot) that estates always retain — front-end candidates, not
embarrassments.

=== 3.2 Configuration management

The four incumbents divide the design space cleanly, and ADR-0002's flagship
demo (Puppet in → Salt out) spans the widest gap:

* *Puppet* — the purest declarative graph: manifests compile to a per-node
  *catalog* whose resources are ordered by `before`/`require`/`notify`/
  `subscribe` edges (the direct source of the meta-model's `Dependency` edge
  vocabulary). An agent converges each node on a cycle; PuppetDB is a
  queryable store of observed state and catalogs — an existing, deployed
  "observed `State` + graph" database. *Exported resources* let one node's
  catalog declare resources realised on another — cross-node dependency, the
  intra-tool ancestor of cross-*tool* handoff.
* *Salt* — both paradigms natively: declarative *states* with requisites
  (`require`, `watch`, `onchanges`) plus imperative execution modules and
  *orchestrate* runbooks, over an event bus with reactors. As the handoff
  *target*, Salt is forgiving precisely because it has both surfaces to land
  on.
* *Ansible* — imperative task lists (explicit order, push-mode) over
  *idempotent modules*, with declarative texture recovered via check mode
  (plan preview again), handlers (`notify` again), and inventory/facts
  (observed state). Its enormous module library is effectively a catalogue of
  per-operator effect models — raw material for the per-op effect model that
  lifting requires.
* *Chef / CFEngine* — convergent resources embedded in an imperative host
  language (Chef), and promise-theoretic autonomous convergence (CFEngine):
  the historical sources of the convergence doctrine 1.3 discussed.

The federation problem is visible in the field: real estates run two or more
of these simultaneously (plus Terraform), with ownership divided by folklore
("the DB hosts are Puppet's, don't let Ansible touch them"). That folklore is
an informal `Owner`/`HandoffBoundary` assignment nobody can query or check —
the meta-model's job is to make it a datum.

=== 3.3 Orchestration and continuous convergence

Kubernetes-style controllers close the loop the one-shot tools leave open:
desired state lives in an API server, and a *reconciler* runs lowering
continuously — observe, diff, act, forever. GitOps (Flux, Argo CD) puts the
desired state in git, which quietly solves provenance (commits, authors,
review trails) for the slice of the world it covers. The meta-model should
treat "reconciliation" not as a different kind of tool but as a *policy on
frequency*: a one-shot apply and a controller loop run the *same* lowered
plan shape on different cadences. That framing keeps the IR unified across
Parts II and III.

=== 3.4 Harnesses

"Harness" covers the machinery that *invokes* automation and *judges* it —
the layer HAR itself lives in:

* *CI/CD pipelines* (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, Jenkins) — imperative DAGs
  of steps with triggers, environments, and approvals. Two meta-model
  contributions: *gated edges* (a `Dependency` edge conditioned on an
  approval — change windows and manual gates are enterprise reality), and
  *artefact provenance* — the supply-chain world (SLSA, signed attestations)
  has already built interchange formats for "what produced this and how",
  which the `Provenance` schema should align with rather than duplicate.
* *Test harnesses* — the setup → exercise → observe → teardown cycle, i.e. a
  plan whose purpose is *observation*, executed against a `State` that must
  be restored afterwards. Expressible as a plan with a mandatory compensating
  suffix — worth keeping expressible, since verification runs (2A's
  round-trip gate) have exactly this shape.
* *RPA* (rpa-elysium, HAR's first-class target) — imperative UI-level
  automation: brittle, long-running, checkpoint-hungry, and rarely
  idempotent. RPA is the *stress case* for handoff — a half-finished bot run
  is a partially-realised estate in miniature — and having it as a native
  `AutomationTarget` keeps the meta-model honest about non-idempotent,
  non-declarative effects that still need `State`, `Owner`, and resumability.

=== 3.5 What "enterprise" actually adds

Comparing Parts II and III, the delta is not sophistication of solving —
package managers have the *hardest* solves — but the social layer:

[%autowidth]
|===
|Enterprise concern |Meta-model home

|Fleet scale and partial application (some hosts converged, some not)
|`State` per resource *per scope*, not per estate

|Team and tool ownership boundaries
|`Owner`, `HandoffBoundary` as queryable data, not folklore

|Compliance and audit ("who changed what, why, under which approval")
|`Provenance` — load-bearing, aligned with supply-chain attestation formats

|Drift (reality diverging from record)
|desired/observed/realised as three distinct `State` facets

|Change control (windows, approvals, plan review)
|plans as persistent, reviewable, approvable interchange objects

|Handoff between tools and teams
|linear ownership transfer + checkpoint (Ephapax-typed)
|===

Single-host tools need almost none of this because a host has one owner and
one journal. The meta-model's distinctive concepts — the ones with no
counterpart in apt — are exactly the enterprise ones, which is the right
sanity check: the interlingua earns its keep at the seams.

== Part IV — Consequences for the meta-model

Empty and weak cells are the product surface: no surveyed tool fills the
`HandoffBoundary` column across a *tool* boundary — that cell is HAR's.

=== 4.2 Design seams the survey mandates

. *Pluggable solve* (1.2) — lowering declares the constraint vocabulary it
  needs; toposort for edge-only graphs, SAT-class where disjunction/conflict
  appear. Follow apt's EDSP precedent: the solver is behind a protocol, not
  welded in.
. *Explanations in plans* (1.2, 2.2) — a plan carries why-this-plan
  (solver derivation) and, on failure, why-no-plan. Candidate plans and
  rejections are representable (aptitude's interaction shape).
. *Prover obligations per operator* (1.3) — each primitive op in the low
  IR declares idempotence/convergence claims; the Idris2 track checks them
  where possible, records them as assumptions where not.
. *Intent provenance* (2.2) — every resource records whether it is directly
  intended or derived (aptitude's auto/manual), enabling GC of derived
  state.
. *Journal + inverse plans as first-class* (2.3, 2.4) — the transaction
  journal is part of the interchange dialect; rollback vocabulary covers all
  four recovery semantics (repair-forward, inverse plan, checkpoint restore,
  atomic switch) without privileging one.
. *Scoped state* (1.4) — `State` is always relative to a scope (shell,
  project, host, fleet); partial application is representable, not an error.
. *Cadence as policy* (3.3) — one-shot apply and continuous reconciliation
  are the same plan under different scheduling policy.
. *Gated dependencies and attestation-aligned provenance* (3.4) — approval
  gates are edge attributes; `Provenance` aligns with existing supply-chain
  attestation formats instead of inventing a parallel scheme.
. *Non-idempotent effects are admitted, not denied* (3.4) — RPA-class
  operations get honest typing (no idempotence claim, checkpoint-required)
  rather than being forced into a convergence costume.

=== 4.3 What this survey does *not* conclude

No finding above implies building a better apt, Terraform, or Puppet.
Each family is treated as a *front-end/back-end candidate and a source of
design precedent*. The strongest single precedent is also the humblest:
`asdf` federated a fragmented family with a four-verb meta-model and
wrapper-not-replacement posture, and it worked. That is the existence proof,
at miniature scale, for the whole programme.
