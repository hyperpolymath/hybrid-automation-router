# 2. Automation Interlingua — Declarative/Imperative Duality and Cross-Tool Handoff

Date: 2026-07-06

## Status

Proposed (owner ratification pending)

## Context

HAR today is, in effect, an *imperative execution backend*: `har-router`
selects targets by strategy and `har-dispatch` runs the dispatch with retry
and dead-letter handling. It takes actions and delivers them. It has no way to
accept a *declarative* description of desired routing state, nor to translate
between the two paradigms.

Two problems motivate a larger direction.

### Problem 1 (acute, near-term): HAR has no declarative/imperative duality of its own

The automation world is split between two control paradigms:

- **Declarative** — describe the desired end-state (Terraform HCL, Puppet
  catalogs, declarative Ansible, Salt states, Nix). Convergence-based,
  idempotent, order-inferred.
- **Imperative** — describe the ordered sequence of actions (Ansible task
  lists, Salt orchestrate, shell, HAR's current dispatch). Order-sensitive.

HAR can only do the imperative half. It cannot take "here is the routing
topology I want" and compute the plan to realise it, nor infer the converged
end-state from a sequence of dispatches. This is the *most acute* gap: until
HAR can do declarative<->imperative translation **on its own routing domain,
standalone**, it cannot credibly do it for anything else.

### Problem 2 (second, longer-term): heterogeneous automation estates do not join up

Real organisations run a bit of Puppet here, a bit of Salt there, some
Terraform, some Ansible — islands that do not interoperate. The goal is **not
to replace** these tools (though replacement may become an eventual, optional
consequence) but to **join up the fragments**: hand a partially-realised
estate off from one tool to another (Puppet in, Salt out), resume it
elsewhere, and present the whole as one coherent, federated view with HAR as
the seam.

### What kind of artefact is this?

Three candidate framings were considered, and they resolve at different
altitudes rather than competing:

- An **intermediate representation (IR)** — transient, single-owner,
  execution-oriented, internal to a compiler. This describes HAR *executing*
  the representation directly.
- An **interlingua / interchange format** — persistent, tool-neutral,
  multi-owner; independent systems read and write it to exchange meaning. This
  describes the *handoff and federation* role.
- A **meta-language** — a language for describing *other* languages/systems.
  This describes the *schema*: a tool-neutral meta-model of automation onto
  which each tool's concepts are mapped.

The conclusion: it is **an interlingua, whose schema is a meta-model of
automation, carried on A2ML, that doubles as an execution IR when HAR runs it
natively.** All three framings are facets of one artefact.

## Decision

Adopt a staged programme to give HAR (1) its own declarative/imperative
capability, then (2) cross-tool handoff and federation, converging on (3) a
lingua franca for automation control. The carrier substrate is **A2ML**.

### The core artefacts

1. **The automation meta-model** — a tool-neutral ontology of automation
   concepts: `Resource`, `Effect`, `Dependency` (`before`/`require`/`notify`),
   `State` (desired / observed / realised), `Owner`, `Provider`,
   `HandoffBoundary`, `Provenance`. This is the "meta-language". Each external
   tool's front-end maps its own concepts onto this meta-model.

2. **A2ML interchange dialect** — an A2ML schema that carries the meta-model.
   NOTE: A2ML today is a *metadata / state-checkpoint* format (STATE.a2ml,
   META.a2ml). Using it as an *active interchange and plan carrier* is a new
   role and requires a dedicated dialect/schema, not reuse of existing
   conventions as-is.

3. **The two-level IR** —
   - *High level (declarative):* an effect/resource graph — nodes are desired
     resources/routes with attributes; edges are dependencies.
   - *Low level (imperative):* a plan — a partially-ordered list of idempotent
     primitive ops (create/update/delete/dispatch/notify) with pre/post
     conditions.
   - **Lowering** (graph -> plan) is the `terraform plan` move: build the DAG,
     topologically sort, emit the apply plan. HAR's existing engine executes
     it.
   - **Lifting** (plan -> graph) is the inverse: infer converged desired-state
     from an action sequence. Lossy; requires a per-op effect model.

4. **Provenance & handoff engine** — because handoff/resume make provenance
   load-bearing, every resource/effect carries *who owns it now* and *its
   realisation status* (`declared -> planned -> realised -> handed-off`).
   Handoff is a **linear ownership transfer with a checkpoint**. This is where
   the currently-unwired formal stack earns its place:
   - **Ephapax linear/affine types** (`src/abi/LinearRouting.eph`) guarantee
     exactly-once ownership — no two tools double-apply the same resource
     across a handoff.
   - **proven-FSM** (`src/abi/ProvenFSM.idr`) governs the per-resource
     lifecycle.
   - **Idris2 invariants** (`RoutingInvariants.idr`) carry idempotence and
     convergence proofs.

### Formalisation strategy: parallel tracks

The Rust IR (for execution) and an Idris2 specification of the meta-model (as
the normative reference) are developed **alongside each other and kept in
sync**. The Rust model is the executable artefact; the Idris2 spec is the
source of truth for the meta-model's shape and invariants. This finally wires
in the ABI/FFI scaffold that currently sits decorative.

### Sequencing (risk-ordered)

- **2A first — HAR's own declarative/imperative core.** Meta-model + A2ML
  dialect + two-level IR + lowering/lifting, proven end-to-end **on HAR's own
  routing domain with no external tool involved**: a declarative route
  topology -> plan -> execute -> lift back to declarative, round-tripping
  through A2ML. This is the acute problem and the de-risking gate for
  everything after it.
- **2B second — external tool adapters and federation.** Front-ends (tool ->
  IR) and back-ends (IR -> tool or direct execution). Flagship demo:
  **Puppet-in -> A2ML -> Salt-out** with a partially-realised estate handed
  off and resumed. Thin Terraform front-end + Ansible slice prove the IR is
  genuinely bidirectional (per the "both, minimal slice" decision). Federation
  view stitches fragments from multiple tools into one graph with
  overlap/conflict detection on ownership boundaries.
- **Long-term — lingua franca.** With 2A+2B mature, HAR's own surface router
  language (human-authored syntax over the meta-model, A2ML-backed) becomes
  the lingua franca; `N` front-ends + `M` back-ends replace `N x M` point
  translators. Replacement of existing tools is an optional, emergent
  consequence, never a precondition.

### Scope and the replacement question (decided)

Active scope is **2A and 2B**: HAR's own declarative/imperative capability and
cross-tool handoff/federation. **Replacement** of incumbent tools is explicitly
**out of scope** for this open project and will not be pursued speculatively.
Rationale (owner, 2026-07-06): the ecosystem fight is not worth taking on
unfunded, and any full realisation is more likely to happen as a commercial
target within one of the incumbent vendors than as a solo open-source campaign.

The replacement track is therefore **parked and sponsor/contributor-gated**:

- It will be revisited only if a sponsor funds it, or a contributor takes it on.
- Until then the foundation sits here, usable by anyone under the project's
  licence (MPL-2.0 for code); the door is explicitly left open.
- This posture is scoped to the *replacement* ambition **only**. The project
  itself and its 2A/2B tracks remain **active**, not dormant.

### Roadmap impact

Per the reprioritisation decision, this becomes the new **Phase 2**. The
former Phase 2 (advanced routing — ML scoring, adaptive load balancing,
circuit breakers) moves to Phase 3, and subsequent phases renumber. Advanced
routing is reframed as a *strategy concern within* the execution backend that
the IR lowers onto.

## Consequences

### Positive

- HAR gains a genuine declarative surface, not just imperative dispatch.
- The formal-methods stack (Ephapax, proven-FSM, Idris2) stops being
  decorative and becomes load-bearing exactly where it is strongest
  (exactly-once ownership, lifecycle, invariants).
- The federation/handoff value proposition ("join up what you already have")
  is differentiated from every "yet another IaC tool" — it does not demand
  migration.
- A2ML gets a second, higher-value role as an interchange substrate, deepening
  estate coherence.
- Risk is ordered correctly: the duality is proven on HAR's own simple domain
  before being claimed as universal.

### Negative

- Large scope expansion; Phase 2 as reprioritised delays advanced routing.
- Lifting (imperative -> declarative) is genuinely hard and lossy; the effect
  model per operation is open research, not just engineering.
- A2ML-as-interchange is an unproven role for the format; the dialect design
  is a real cost and a potential source of estate-wide churn.
- Keeping the Rust IR and Idris2 spec in sync is ongoing discipline, not a
  one-off.
- Each external tool front-end/back-end is a substantial, independently
  maintained surface with its own version-drift exposure.

### Neutral

- The existing crates (`har-core`, `har-router`, `har-dispatch`, etc.) are
  retained as the execution backend the IR lowers onto; nothing is thrown
  away.
- Naming of the interlingua / router language is deferred to a follow-up
  owner decision (working name: "automation interlingua").
- Replacement of incumbent tools is decided out-of-scope and
  sponsor/contributor-gated (see "Scope and the replacement question"); the
  open foundation remains usable under the project licence. This scoping
  applies to the replacement ambition only — the project stays active.
